Seventeen Point Eight Million: IOM's 2025 Reach and the Budget That's Contracting

IOM provided humanitarian assistance to 17.8 million people across 170 countries and territories in 2025 — shelter for 7.3 million and 4.2 million health consultations — even as global funding contracted and needs reached unprecedented levels.

By Ummanitarian Insight Editorial Team · August 2026 · 4 min read · Field Ops

Humanitarian aid distribution at Khazer frontline camp for displaced people in northern Iraq — the kind of assistance IOM and partner agencies deliver in displacement crises. Photo: Mstyslav Chernov, CC BY-SA 4.0.

On 5 August, the International Organization for Migration released its Annual Report 2025: humanitarian assistance reaching 17.8 million people across 170 countries and territories in a single year — a figure delivered, the agency stresses, while global funding for humanitarian work continued to contract.

The report shows where that reach was concentrated. IOM focused its operations on crisis-affected settings including Gaza, Haiti, Myanmar, Ukraine and the Sahel, where it provided shelter assistance to 7.3 million people during the year. The agency also delivered 4.2 million primary health consultations and supported 5.1 million people with water, sanitation and hygiene services.

Beyond direct assistance, the organisation's data informed 21 humanitarian response plans, helping governments and aid agencies decide where to target support. The report frames this as coordination and evidence-based intervention: in a year when resources shrank, deciding precisely where each dollar goes became part of the response itself.

That combination — record needs against contracting budgets — runs through the agency's own summary of the year. "Conflicts, disasters, disease outbreaks and displacement drove needs to unprecedented levels, even as resources contracted dramatically," said Ugochi Daniels, IOM's deputy director general for operations. "In spite of these challenges, IOM remained present where people needed us most."

The funding gap is not unique to IOM. Agencies across the humanitarian system entered 2026 reporting response plans funded at a fraction of assessed need, and IOM's own announcement of the report's findings was framed as delivery "despite growing humanitarian funding gaps". The 2025 numbers, in other words, describe what a shrinking budget can still do — and raise the question of what the next year's report will show if the trend continues.

Ummanitarian Perspective

Seventeen point eight million people is the largest footprint any migration agency has reported in a single year — and it was reached with resources the report itself describes as dramatically contracted. The achievement deserves the headline; the trend behind it deserves the scrutiny. When needs rise to unprecedented levels while funding shrinks, the gap is not merely financial — it is a forecast. The 2025 report documents what a stretched system can still deliver. The next report will show what happens when the stretching has to stop somewhere.
