Three Hundred Thirty-Three Thousand Patients: Why Are Indonesia's Doctors Marking Their Day in the Haze?

The medical association's 76th anniversary fell in the week that smoke from land fires pushed respiratory cases past 333,000 in seven provinces, with 75,172 of them children under five.

By Ummanitarian Insight Editorial Team · October 2026 · 4 min read · Health & Displacement

Haze over a river crossing in Banjarmasin, South Kalimantan, on the morning of 30 August 2026. Photo: Ilham Mufti Laksono / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0.

On 24 October 2026 the Indonesian Medical Association (IDI) marked seventy-six years since its founding, and the press conference at its Jakarta secretariat was about smoke. The association's anniversary fell inside a respiratory emergency that the Ministry of Health had already put on the record.

According to figures released through the national disaster agency on 22 October, health facilities recorded 333,470 accumulated cases of acute respiratory infection, known in Indonesia as ISPA, in areas affected by land and forest fires between 18 August and 21 October 2026. Of that total, 258,298 were patients aged five and over, and 75,172 were children under five. “The spread of recorded ISPA cases covers 68 districts and cities in seven provinces,” said Then Suyanti, the ministry's director of environmental health, in the release. She said the steep rise tracked the thickening haze.

At the anniversary event, IDI chair Slamet Budiarto described the smoke as more than an irritant. According to Kompas, he said prolonged exposure can produce respiratory and lung infection and, over the long term, cancer, and pointed to air quality in affected cities, naming Palembang, as having reached unhealthy and hazardous categories. He asked the government to treat the issue as urgent. He also said no significant reports of illness among health workers had reached the association, and that doctors were continuing to see patients in masks.

Hospitals describe pressure at the point where the system meets patients. Iing Ichsan Hanafi, chair of the Indonesian Private Hospitals Association, said outpatient visits for respiratory cases had risen noticeably during the fires, and that the association was coordinating with provincial health offices in the affected areas. Some hospitals, he said, were distributing multivitamins to their own staff so that service could continue. He did not give a figure for the increase.

The date has a longer history than the emergency. IDI was established on 24 October 1950, five years after the proclamation of independence and months after sovereignty was transferred at the end of 1949, according to ANTARA. The anniversary is observed as National Doctors' Day. An organisation founded when the country had too few physicians now spends its anniversary discussing air quality, which is a change in the job description as much as in the calendar.

The arithmetic of the caseload is what makes the day concrete. Just over one in five of the patients counted this season is a child under five: 75,172 out of 333,470. The figures are clinic records, which means they measure people who reached care. Those who stayed at home, or who live beyond the reach of a health centre, are not in the number.

The ministry's count runs to 21 October. Whether the curve has turned will show in the next release, and in the outpatient queues that the hospitals association says are still busy. For an association that measures its years partly in greetings, the harder question is what would count as a good year: fewer hectares burned before the season starts, or fewer patients registered after it begins.

Tiga hari sebelumnya, Kementerian Kesehatan mencatat 333.470 kasus kumulatif infeksi saluran pernapasan akut (ISPA) di wilayah terdampak kebakaran hutan dan lahan sejak 18 Agustus hingga 21 Oktober 2026, meliputi 68 kabupaten/kota di tujuh provinsi. Sebanyak 258.298 kasus terjadi pada penduduk berusia di atas lima tahun dan 75.172 pada anak di bawah lima tahun, atau lebih dari satu dari lima pasien. Ketua Umum PB IDI Slamet Budiarto menyatakan paparan asap berkepanjangan dapat memicu infeksi pernapasan, infeksi paru, dan dalam jangka panjang kanker, serta menyoroti kualitas udara di sejumlah kota terdampak, termasuk Palembang, yang masuk kategori tidak sehat hingga berbahaya. Ketua Umum ARSSI Iing Ichsan Hanafi menyebut kunjungan rawat jalan kasus ISPA meningkat, sejumlah rumah sakit memberi multivitamin kepada tenaga kesehatan agar pelayanan tetap berjalan, dan asosiasi berkoordinasi dengan dinas kesehatan provinsi. Angka Kementerian Kesehatan berhenti pada 21 Oktober, sehingga arah kurva baru terlihat pada rilis berikutnya.

Ummanitarian Perspective

The health sector is the last institution to receive a land-use problem, and the first to be judged on how it handled the consequences. That ordering explains why an anniversary conversation moved so quickly from gratitude to air quality. Fires are decided by land clearing, drainage and enforcement; the patients arrive at clinics in provinces where those decisions were taken months earlier, and the bill is settled in consultations that no one budgets for in advance.

Two gaps in the record are worth naming. Case counts built on clinic attendance measure access as much as exposure: a district with fewer functioning health centres will record fewer patients for the same air, and its low number will be read as good news. Pairing the two records would close the second gap, so that a case count is published beside the district air reading that produced it, in time for a family to decide whether a child should go to school that morning. A season counted that way would show whether the 75,172 children were reached early or late, which is the only measure of the year that matters after the greetings are done.
